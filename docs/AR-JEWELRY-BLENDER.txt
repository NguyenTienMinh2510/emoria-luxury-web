REST / RUNTIME
GET /api/v1/products/:id/try-on verifies the product is active, returns the matching GLB and calibration.
server/tryOn.js: curated 7 designs, supported duo aliases, optional details.ar overrides.
Model URLs must be same-origin .glb paths. enabled:false disables AR.
Products without a matching 3D asset no longer display an unrelated generic design.
Horizon is a bracelet; Halo/Echo are rings; Lumen/Faith/Sen use necklace placement.
For Sen this is a charm suspended on a simulated chain, not a claim that a chain is included in the order.
GLB meters; +Y up, +Z ornament front; ring hole axis Y. Dimensions are inferred, not measured.
Necklace chain is a separate instanced 3D mesh, adjusted to shoulder span and simulated length.
Ring/wrist depth masks are geometric approximations. No hair/clothing segmentation.
Hand orientation is optimized for a frontal view; the same model is displayed on either hand.

PERFORMANCE
Only the selected GLB is loaded when camera starts; Three.js remains in lazy AR chunks.
~0.26–1.88 MB per new GLB, roughly 8,900–65,600 triangles.
Meshes joined by material; necklace uses 160 links in one instanced draw, rebuilt only when length changes.
Camera rendering capped around 30 fps; tracking requested at about 12 fps in a worker.
Model scale responds to landmark span and size/placement controls.
Camera tracks, workers, GPU buffers, environment maps and photo blob URLs released on close.
Late model/camera results are discarded after close; missing GLB/WebGL produces a visible error.

VISUAL LIMITS
These are modeled interpretations of single reference photos, not exact scans or manufacturing CAD.
Back sides, depth, chain wrap and dimensions are inferred. Preview is not a ring-size measurement.
Crystal material is a real-time approximation; it does not simulate diamond caustics or spectral dispersion.
Desktop browser and synthetic video tests do not establish tracking accuracy or frame rate on a physical iPhone.
